Factorial finder

n! is the product n · (n-1) · … · 1, and 0! is 1. Computed with a loop, with recursion, and with a fast product tree — all in exact BigInt arithmetic, because 21! already overflows a JavaScript number. It also tells you the trailing zeros and the digit count without ever building the number, and runs the sum backwards. All offline: nothing you type leaves the page.

Try one:

    n! =

    Divides out 1, 2, 3, … — a factorial peels cleanly to 1.

    Factorials grow ferociously: 13! is past a 32-bit int, 21! is past Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER, and 100! has 158 digits a float can only show as 9.33e157. That is why the value here is an exact BigInt, never a floating-point number.
