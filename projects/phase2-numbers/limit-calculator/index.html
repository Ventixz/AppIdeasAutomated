Limit calculator

Type a function of x and a point to approach, and read back the limit — evaluated numerically by creeping in from both sides and accelerating the guess with Richardson extrapolation. It tells you not just the value but why: whether the two sides agree, whether it runs off to ±∞, or whether it simply doesn't exist. All offline: nothing you type leaves the page.

    How it works

    A limit asks what f(x) heads toward as x nears a point — not what it equals there, which may be undefined (sin(x)/x at 0 is 0/0). So the calculator never evaluates at the point. It samples f at a shrinking geometric ladder of offsets h = 0.125, 0.0625, … on each side, then feeds those samples to Neville/Richardson extrapolation, which fits a polynomial in h and reads off its value at h = 0. That turns a slow crawl into a sharp estimate.

    The two sides are then compared. Agree → the limit exists. Both blow up with the same sign → ±∞. Disagree, or oscillate without settling (sin(1/x)) → the limit does not exist. Limits at ∞ are handled by substituting x = 1/t and taking t → 0⁺. Type inf or -inf as the point to try them.

    Syntax: + - * / ^, parentheses, and implicit multiplication (2x, 3(x+1)). Functions: sin cos tan, asin…, sinh…, exp, ln/log (natural), log10, log2, sqrt, cbrt, abs. Constants: pi, e, tau, phi.
