Happy numbers

Take a number, replace it with the sum of the squares of its digits, and repeat. Land on 1 and the number is happy; fall into a loop that never reaches 1 and it is unhappy. This traces the whole path, plots it, and — because the process shrinks any number to something tiny in one step — classifies inputs with thousands of digits. All offline: nothing you type leaves the page.

Why it always terminates

A d-digit number's digit-square-sum is at most 81·d. So a 4-digit number maps to at most 324, and after one step every number — however huge — is already below 1000. From there the sequence wanders a finite set, so it must eventually repeat: either it hits the fixed point 1 (happy), or it enters a cycle (unhappy). In base 10 with squares there is exactly one unhappy cycle — 4 → 16 → 37 → 58 → 89 → 145 → 42 → 20 → 4 — so being happy is the same as not reaching that loop. This page detects the cycle directly rather than assuming it, which is why it also works for other bases and powers.
