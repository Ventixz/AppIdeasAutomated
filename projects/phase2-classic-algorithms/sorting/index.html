Why some are faster

The three simple sorts — bubble, insertion, selection — compare roughly n²/2 pairs, so doubling the array quadruples the work. The three fast ones — merge, quick and heap — do about n log n comparisons by never re-examining pairs they've already resolved. On 2,000 items that's the difference between ~2 million comparisons and ~22,000. But the simple sorts aren't useless: insertion sort is linear on nearly-sorted data (try the "nearly sorted" shape), which is why real-world hybrid sorts fall back to it for small or almost-ordered runs.

Watch which sorts are stable: bubble, insertion and merge never reorder equal keys; selection, quick and heap can. And notice quicksort's median-of-three pivot — it keeps the "already sorted" and "reversed" shapes from triggering its O(n²) worst case.
